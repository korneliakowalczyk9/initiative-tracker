# Initiative Tracker Product Requirements

## Status and scope notes

This document records the current product brief at the discovery stage. A requirement being confirmed as part of the brief does not mean it is approved for the MVP. Unless identified as a decision below, feature scope and priority are **unprioritised / not yet agreed**. Recommendations are proposals for discovery, not decisions.

Use fictional examples and data only. Do not include confidential workplace information.

## Confirmed requirements

The following statements are confirmed in the project brief. Their inclusion in an agreed MVP remains to be prioritised with the product owner.

### Purpose and users

- The product is intended to be a standalone web application for registering, managing and monitoring initiatives within a defined organizational scope.
- It addresses common limitations in existing tracking tools and explores how teams can maintain operational updates alongside broader portfolio reporting.
- The intended user population includes a coordination group managing initiatives, initiative owners, decision owners for the defined scope and designated contacts providing updates.
- Users have different levels of technical confidence. The experience should be straightforward and accessible to less technically experienced users.

### Initiative types and information

- The product should support AI-related initiatives, process improvements, technology implementations, data-sharing initiatives and other operational initiatives; it must not be designed exclusively around regulatory projects.
- The brief identifies these initiative fields: initiative number and name, date entered into the system, go-live date, initiative owner, scope-level decision owner, designated contact, current status, time in current status, total elapsed time since work started, risks and relevant comments.
- A sector field is not currently prioritized for the initial version.

### Status, timers and history

- Workflow stage and health (the initiative's reported delivery condition) are independent. Changing an initiative's workflow stage must not automatically change its health. Health changes only when a new assessment of the initiative's condition is made. Update freshness is independent of both.
- A new health assessment is prompted during each scheduled update, when the initiative owner reviews and confirms current health and when a material change could affect delivery (for example, a new risk or blocker, a missed milestone, a dependency issue, or resolution of an existing problem). After a material change, the initiative owner is responsible for reviewing its impact and submitting an updated health assessment. The decision owner is the primary and only confirmed stakeholder who may flag a material change or raise a concern to prompt that owner review. The initiative owner owns assessment and is not an additional stakeholder flagger; the internal coordination team is not a material-change flagger. A flag or concern prompts owner review only and does not itself change health. The initiative owner remains accountable for reviewing the impact and submitting an updated health assessment. Only an explicit assessment changes health. If no new assessment is submitted, the last reported health is retained and update freshness is tracked independently. Automatic detection of every material event is not assumed. How flags are submitted, routed to the initiative owner, or otherwise surfaced, and the notification mechanism and technical implementation remain open; no email channel or automatic routing is assumed.
- Health remains at its last reported value (for example, **On track**) when an update becomes overdue. An overdue update must not automatically change health to **At risk** or **Blocked**; missing information and delivery problems are different.
- The interface must identify displayed health as coming from the last submitted update and show when that update was received, so stale health is not presented as verified current. The health value list is not approved.
- **Overdue** is a confirmed freshness label, not a health value. **Awaiting update** is neither a health value nor a confirmed freshness label. Other exact freshness labels, the cutoff and what clears the overdue state remain unresolved.
- The exact workflow stages and health value list remain open.
- Proposed health examples and meanings remain unapproved: **On track** (progressing as expected), **At risk** (a risk or potential delay has been reported), **Blocked** (progress cannot continue because of an unresolved issue) and **Completed** (the initiative is complete). This does not approve a health value list.
- The specific-status candidate list is not confirmed or exhaustive. **New** is the confirmed initial specific-status label for an initiative that has been created but not yet submitted to the reviewing organization, followed by **Under review**, the decision-review stage. If the reviewing organization accepts the initiative for implementation, the next specific status is **In progress**. This is the specified status mapping and does not assert that implementation work has already started. If the reviewing organization decides not to implement, the local initiative status is **Withdrawn**: the initiative will not be implemented for the defined scope, and this does not cancel or alter the portfolio-level initiative. This application cannot cancel the portfolio-level initiative; no portfolio-level edit rights or synchronization behavior are implied. If no decision is made within the defined review window, the initiative should be escalated to the **decision owner** and the internal coordination team; both are confirmed recipients. This missed-decision escalation is distinct from a negative implementation decision and from the separate scheduled update-freshness rule. The delivery method, escalation content, whether escalation runs automatically, the window start and working-day calendar and timing details beyond the missed decision window remain unresolved. Missing the decision deadline alone does not mean the initiative is **Blocked**. **To do** is not used as a separate status. **Additional information required** (further input is needed) and **Completed** (work has finished) remain proposed values; the workflow after further assessment is unresolved. Whether voluntary local withdrawal is a distinct outcome from a negative implementation decision remains open only if that distinction is meaningful to the workflow. No further sequence or transition rules, or use of the same stages for every initiative type, are confirmed. Exact overall health values and other workflow stages remain open. This status decision does not approve the feature for the MVP.
- Status changes should be logged with a timestamp. The timestamp changes only when a status is explicitly updated, not merely as time elapses. How changes to the two dimensions are separately recorded remains to be validated.
- Scheduled status updates are due by an agreed reporting deadline. If no update is received by the agreed deadline, the record must automatically turn red and freshness is **Overdue**. A missing update means progress is unknown and the team should follow up; it does not by itself mean the initiative is **At risk** or **Blocked**, or change the last reported health. The interface must show when the health update was received. The exact update cutoff and time zone, what qualifies as an update, what event clears the overdue/red indication and the technical representation remain open.
- **Current-status duration:** starts when an initiative enters a status, resets whenever that status changes and records the date and time of every transition. Which of the two status dimensions this timer applies to, how updates to one dimension affect it and whether it stops on local withdrawal with its final value retained remain open questions.
- **Total elapsed time:** continues across later status changes and stops when an initiative is either **Completed** or locally **Withdrawn**. Its final value must be retained in archived records for both outcomes. The start point remains unresolved: the earlier brief says the first active status, while the product owner says tracking starts when the initiative is sent/submitted to the reviewing organization. These start points must not be conflated; sending/submission is an event, not a specific-status value.
- Status history must be retained so users can review prior statuses and the time spent in each stage. How history represents the two status dimensions and other timer behavior requires validation; the total elapsed-time terminal behavior is specified above.

### Requested functionality

The brief requests the following capabilities. They are **unprioritised / not yet agreed as MVP scope**:

- Registering and editing initiatives.
- A central initiative table, search and filtering.
- An overview of active initiatives.
- Assigning initiative owners and designated contacts.
- The primary path for confirming the reviewing organization's implementation decision is for the decision owner, or a designated contact acting on the decision owner's behalf, to confirm or update the decision/status directly in the tracker. If the decision arrives by email, the internal coordination team may record it manually in the application; email is optional, and there is no assumed automatic email ingestion or status change. After confirmation, the delivery team may update the initiative's workflow status. An accepted implementation decision maps to **In progress**; a negative decision not to implement sets the local initiative status to **Withdrawn**, meaning it will not be implemented for the defined scope and does not cancel or alter the portfolio-level initiative. This application cannot cancel the portfolio-level initiative; no portfolio-level edit rights or synchronization behavior are implied. This is distinct from no decision within the agreed review period, which triggers escalation. This two-path decision-confirmation and status-update behavior is confirmed, not an MVP approval. The status mapping does not assert implementation work has already started. How the decision is evidenced and how status timer/history represent these outcomes remain unresolved. The workflow after further assessment is open. Health reassessment remains the initiative owner's responsibility and is separate from workflow-status updates. Permissions beyond these named workflow actors, audit behavior and technical implementation remain open.
- Status management and status-history review.
- Recording risks.
- Notifications and reminders.
- Escalation to the **decision owner** and the internal coordination team when the reviewing organization has not decided whether to implement within the defined **Under review** period. Both are confirmed recipients. The delivery method, escalation content, whether escalation runs automatically and timing details beyond the missed decision window remain unresolved. This requested capability remains unprioritised / not yet agreed as MVP scope. This missed-decision escalation is distinct from the scheduled update-freshness rule.
- An archive of completed and locally **Withdrawn** initiatives, with the ability to retrieve and review archived initiatives. Users can filter **Completed** and **Withdrawn** initiatives separately. A locally **Withdrawn** initiative leaves the active list and is retained in the same archive as completed initiatives; this local disposition does not cancel or alter the portfolio-level initiative. The archive must preserve the distinction between **Completed** (work has finished) and local **Withdrawn** (the initiative will not be implemented for the defined scope). This requested capability remains unprioritised / not yet agreed as MVP scope.

Notification examples named in the brief are new initiative assignments, status changes, requests for updates, reminders when an initiative has not been updated, risks and escalations. Automatic email notifications are a desired capability.

### Experience and product-development principles

- The interface should be simple, intuitive, easy to navigate and focused on operationally relevant information.
- It should be less cluttered than the existing tracking tools, provide a clear overview and easy initiative updates and avoid unnecessary fields, complicated navigation and features without operational value.
- This is a learning project as well as a product-development project. The product owner wants to participate in decisions and understand the technical implementation.
- Recommendations should be explained, assumptions challenged, work done incrementally, unnecessary complexity avoided and requirements distinguished from assumptions and suggestions.
- Application implementation must not begin until the initial product requirements and MVP scope have been agreed.

## Suggested improvements

The following are product-management recommendations for discovery, not agreed requirements or decisions:

- **Personas:** clarify each user group's goals, responsibilities, access needs and technical confidence. Use evidence about day-to-day burden to guide which workflows to understand first.
- **Workflow mapping:** map how an initiative is registered, updated, reviewed, escalated, completed and retrieved from the archive, including who acts at each step.
- **Health, workflow stage and freshness:** validate proposed health values and workflow stages separately. The initiative owner is responsible for reviewing the impact of material changes and submitting an updated health assessment. The decision owner is the primary and only confirmed stakeholder who can flag a material change or raise a concern to prompt owner review; the initiative owner owns assessment and is not an additional stakeholder flagger, and the internal coordination team is not a flagger. Access controls for flags, how flags reach the owner and the notification mechanism and technical implementation remain open. Do not assume email or automatic detection. Determine how assessments are submitted, stored, displayed and represented technically. Workflow-stage changes do not automatically change health; health changes only when a new assessment is made. Keep freshness distinct from last-reported health; **Overdue** is confirmed as a freshness label, but other labels and cutoff/clearing rules are unresolved. Challenge the assumption that one fixed stage list or linear sequence will fit every initiative type. Clarify which dimension drives each timer and how pauses, reopened work, missing dates and corrections to status history should affect them.
- **Illustrative example only:** a data-sharing initiative could have health “On track” from its last submitted update, freshness “Overdue,” and workflow stage “Additional information required,” with 8 days in the current stage and 23 days total elapsed. The interface would show when that update was received. This illustrates distinct concepts; it does not approve a health value list, freshness rules beyond the confirmed label, a workflow sequence, or timer rules.
- **Red freshness indicator:** consider using red to communicate only that an update is stale or missing, not project health or risk. Do not rely on color alone; pair the color with a clear text or other accessible indicator. This is a suggestion, not a confirmed user requirement.
- **Notification and escalation rules:** define recipients, triggering events, timing, frequency, ownership and expected action before selecting notification channels or automation.
- **MVP prioritisation:** evaluate requested capabilities against user value, effort, dependencies and the need for a usable end-to-end workflow. Explicitly agree what is in and out before implementation begins.
